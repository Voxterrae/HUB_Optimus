# HUB_Optimus Common Good v0.1

**Status:** Public-benefit operating policy proposed under issue `#1863`  
**Principle:** Free to the eligible beneficiary; no profit from suffering

## 1. Purpose

HUB_Optimus Common Good provides selected professional capability without a
professional fee when the work directly serves human welfare, animal welfare,
humanitarian protection, essential rights, prevention of suffering, or another
approved common-good purpose.

The program is financed by commercial strength, not by extracting value from
victims or beneficiaries.

## 2. Eligible work

Examples may include:

- protecting or improving human welfare and dignity;
- animal rescue, welfare, protection, and prevention of cruelty;
- humanitarian coordination and essential-service continuity;
- safeguarding vulnerable people;
- access to reliable information, evidence, or decision support in a clearly
  noncommercial public-benefit context;
- operational automation that lets a legitimate nonprofit direct more resources
  to beneficiaries;
- prevention of avoidable harm where HUB_Optimus has a safe and appropriate
  capability.

Eligibility is never automatic. Every engagement requires a written decision and
scope.

## 3. Eligibility requirements

The applicant or sponsoring organization must demonstrate:

1. legitimate nonprofit, charitable, public-benefit, or mission-locked status;
2. a direct and credible benefit to humans, animals, or an approved common-good
   objective;
3. responsible governance and safeguarding;
4. a lawful and ethical use case;
5. no hidden commercial, reseller, political, electoral, surveillance, or
   reputational beneficiary;
6. appropriate data protection and security;
7. realistic scope and internal capacity to use the result responsibly;
8. acceptance of the noncommercial license and IP boundary.

## 4. Ineligible uses

The following are ineligible:

- public institutions seeking to bypass procurement or budgets;
- commercial companies, consultants, or integrators using an NGO as a free
  delivery channel;
- corporate social-responsibility projects whose principal beneficiary is the
  corporation's reputation, sales, data, or market access;
- political parties, campaigns, covert influence, lobbying, propaganda, or
  voter targeting;
- surveillance, profiling, manipulation, discrimination, or exploitation of
  vulnerable people or animals;
- weapons, coercive control, harmful experimentation, or systems that increase
  avoidable harm;
- resale, white-label, sublicensing, or commercial deployment of the pro bono
  result;
- projects that require unsafe access, unlawful data processing, or authority
  HUB_Optimus does not possess.

## 5. Financial boundary

For an approved scope:

- HUB_Optimus professional fees to the beneficiary are EUR 0;
- unavoidable third-party costs are passed through at cost, with no margin;
- sponsors and grants may cover direct costs or fund additional capacity;
- no percentage of donations is taken;
- no success fee is linked to victims, suffering, illness, displacement, abuse,
  rescue volume, or emergencies;
- no beneficiary publicity, endorsement, data, or story is required as payment.

A sponsor funding a public-benefit engagement does not acquire beneficiary data,
ownership of HUB_Optimus, control over the nonprofit, or commercial rights to the
result unless a separate lawful agreement exists and preserves the public-
benefit purpose.

## 6. Scope and capacity

Each pro bono engagement must define:

- beneficiary and approved purpose;
- start and end date;
- deliverables and exclusions;
- maximum time or capacity;
- data, privacy, security, and safeguarding boundaries;
- infrastructure and third-party costs;
- support and maintenance, if any;
- noncommercial license;
- exit, suspension, and misuse conditions;
- responsible contact and decision-maker.

Free work is not unlimited work. A defined scope protects the beneficiary and
keeps the program sustainable.

## 7. Selection priority

Selection considers:

- expected human or animal benefit;
- urgency and preventability of harm;
- legitimacy and safeguarding quality;
- fit with proven HUB_Optimus capability;
- ability to deliver safely;
- absence of a better-funded responsible alternative;
- available Common Good reserve and capacity;
- opportunity to create a reusable public-benefit pattern without exposing
  private data.

Publicity value is not a selection criterion.

## 8. Data and dignity

HUB_Optimus must minimize data collection and protect dignity.

Without separate, informed, and revocable permission, it must not:

- publish identities, images, case histories, location, health, or vulnerability;
- use beneficiary information for unrelated model training or commercial
  development;
- sell or exchange personal data;
- build marketing around suffering;
- disclose a beneficiary relationship.

Aggregate learning may be retained only when lawful, safely anonymized, and
consistent with the written engagement.

## 9. Intellectual property and license

The eligible beneficiary receives the rights needed for the approved
noncommercial purpose. Unless expressly agreed otherwise:

- HUB_Optimus retains its core platform, architecture, methods, connectors,
  policies, templates, tools, know-how, and reusable improvements;
- the beneficiary may not resell, sublicense, white-label, transfer, or use the
  result for commercial gain;
- third-party licenses remain subject to their own terms;
- termination of the engagement does not erase the beneficiary's lawful rights
  to its own data.

## 10. Misuse and termination

HUB_Optimus may suspend or terminate the pro bono engagement if:

- eligibility information was false or incomplete;
- the work is redirected toward commercial, political, surveillance, harmful, or
  discriminatory use;
- data or safeguarding obligations are breached;
- the beneficiary attempts resale, transfer, or exploitation;
- continuing would create disproportionate legal, safety, security, or ethical
  risk;
- the agreed scope or capacity is exhausted.

Suspension must prioritize safe transition and protection of beneficiaries where
reasonably possible.

## 11. Reserve and reporting

The initial target is 5% of collected net commercial service revenue, or
comparable delivery capacity, for approved Common Good work once commercial
revenue begins.

When the contracting and accounting structure is operational, internal reporting
should distinguish:

- commercial revenue collected;
- reserve generated;
- approved pro bono capacity;
- direct third-party costs;
- beneficiary outcomes reported without exposing protected identities;
- unused reserve carried forward where practical.

This reporting is for accountability, not beneficiary marketing.
