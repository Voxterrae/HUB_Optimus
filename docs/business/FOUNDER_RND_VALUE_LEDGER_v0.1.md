# HUB_Optimus — Founder-Funded R&D and Value Ledger v0.1

**Status:** Management policy proposed under issue `#1863`  
**Founder and owner:** Benjamin Gerrit Hoff

## 1. Construction work is investment, not free client work

During the construction phase, time spent by Benjamin Gerrit Hoff and approved
contributors on HUB_Optimus, LCDH-OS as client zero, Operator, Control Plane,
connectors, governance, tests, documentation, and reusable systems is classified
internally as **founder-funded research and development**.

It may not yet generate an invoice, but it creates:

- intellectual property;
- reusable architecture and software;
- operational knowledge;
- verified capability;
- case-study evidence;
- reduced future delivery cost;
- commercial options and enterprise value.

No external company or institution acquires a right to free work merely because
the capability was developed during the founder-funded construction phase.

## 2. Shadow-value rates

For internal management and pricing evidence, use the following initial shadow
rates:

- Benjamin Gerrit Hoff — founder architecture, product direction, governance,
  strategic design, and final integration: **EUR 2,500 per working day**;
- engineering, implementation, integration, testing, and technical delivery:
  **EUR 1,500 per working day**;
- specialist external work: actual cost or approved market-equivalent rate;
- infrastructure, licenses, legal, accounting, design, and other direct costs:
  actual cost.

A shadow rate is not an invoice, salary, tax value, accounting asset, or legal
valuation. It is a management measure of invested effort and replacement cost.
Formal accounting capitalization or company valuation requires professional
accounting and legal treatment.

## 3. Required ledger entry

Material construction work should record:

- date or period;
- contributor;
- workstream and product;
- issue, PR, commit, decision, or evidence reference;
- hours or days;
- applicable shadow rate;
- calculated shadow value;
- direct cash costs;
- output produced;
- reusable versus client-specific classification;
- current maturity: concept, prototype, tested, pilot-ready, or production-ready;
- owner of the resulting IP;
- customer-zero or public-benefit benefit, if applicable;
- commercial package or future use enabled;
- risks, dependencies, and next validation step.

## 4. Monthly management view

The monthly summary should show:

- founder-funded days and shadow value;
- engineering days and shadow value;
- direct cash expenditure;
- cumulative invested value;
- reusable assets created;
- production or pilot readiness gained;
- unpaid external requests rejected or converted into paid opportunities;
- commercial revenue and cash collected;
- Common Good reserve generated;
- approved pro bono capacity delivered.

This makes the invisible investment visible without pretending that unbilled
work is revenue.

## 5. Client-zero treatment

La Casa – Das Haus / LCDH-OS may serve as the first real operating environment
and client-zero validation case.

Client-zero work should distinguish:

- reusable HUB_Optimus core and methods;
- LCDH-OS-specific configuration and data;
- improvements validated through real operations;
- measurable time, risk, cost, quality, or revenue impact;
- future sector package potential.

Client-zero status does not transfer ownership of the HUB_Optimus core. It
creates evidence and a reference implementation.

## 6. Evidence and commercial conversion

The ledger should support:

- credible proposals and price justification;
- grant, investor, partner, or joint-venture due diligence;
- replacement-cost and contribution analysis;
- product-roadmap decisions;
- identification of reusable packages;
- decisions to stop low-value work;
- proof that a requested external deliverable already embodies substantial
  founder-funded investment.

Do not publish sensitive time, cost, security, customer, or valuation data in the
public repository. Public records may show capability and provenance; detailed
financial ledgers belong in a protected business system.

## 7. Transition from construction to commerce

The operating transition is:

```text
Founder-funded R&D
        ↓
Verified reusable capability
        ↓
Paid diagnostic
        ↓
Paid pilot
        ↓
Paid implementation
        ↓
Recurring license and managed operation
        ↓
Commercial surplus funds further R&D and Common Good
```

External bespoke work must enter through a paid engagement or an explicitly
approved Common Good scope. Construction phase is not a permanent waiver of
commercial value.
