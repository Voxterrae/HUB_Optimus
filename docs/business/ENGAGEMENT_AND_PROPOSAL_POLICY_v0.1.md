# HUB_Optimus — Engagement and Proposal Policy v0.1

## 1. Purpose

This policy converts the commercial and public-benefit philosophy into a repeatable
client intake, proposal, contracting, and exception process.

## 2. Classify the request first

Every request must be placed in one track:

| Track | Default | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial company or funded organization | Paid | Qualification, then paid diagnostic or quotation |
| Enterprise or large corporation | Paid | Minimum EUR 10,000 engagement unless owner exception |
| Public institution or public company | Paid | Procurement-compatible paid engagement |
| Verified qualifying human/animal welfare nonprofit | Common Good candidate | Written eligibility and pro bono scope |
| Research or educational collaboration | Paid unless clearly unfunded and approved as Common Good | Written classification |
| Joint venture or strategic partner | Funded delivery plus written JV terms | No implied rights |
| Unclear or mixed beneficiary | Fail closed | Identify actual beneficiary and budget before work |

## 3. Free qualification boundary

A free introductory call is limited to:

- one call;
- maximum 30 minutes;
- understanding the problem at a high level;
- explaining public HUB_Optimus capability;
- confirming budget, authority, urgency, and fit;
- agreeing the next paid or Common Good step.

Do not provide custom architecture, diagnosis, code, data analysis, tender design,
implementation planning, written recommendations, or a reusable deliverable.

## 4. Required information before a proposal

- legal contracting entity;
- decision-maker and budget owner;
- actual beneficiary and users;
- problem, desired outcome, urgency, and constraints;
- systems, integrations, data classes, and jurisdictions;
- security, privacy, audit, accessibility, and procurement needs;
- scope, deliverables, exclusions, and acceptance criteria;
- requested rights, source-code access, exclusivity, or resale;
- operating and support expectations;
- target budget and procurement timeline;
- classification as commercial, public-sector, Common Good, or joint venture.

## 5. Proposal minimum content

Every proposal must state:

1. client and beneficiary;
2. commercial or Common Good classification;
3. objective and measurable outcome;
4. scope and deliverables;
5. exclusions and dependencies;
6. implementation stages and milestones;
7. price, taxes, expenses, and payment schedule;
8. validity period;
9. data protection, confidentiality, and security boundary;
10. intellectual-property and license boundary;
11. change control;
12. acceptance and support;
13. suspension, termination, and rollback;
14. responsible parties;
15. applicable contract and jurisdiction review.

## 6. Evidence-first sales practice

Use existing proof before building new free proof:

- public repository evidence;
- redacted demonstrations;
- tests, benchmarks, checks, and audit trails;
- architecture diagrams without secrets;
- controlled case studies with permission;
- paid pilot with defined acceptance criteria.

A bespoke demonstration that requires real design or engineering is a paid
mini-engagement or part of a paid diagnostic.

## 7. Change requests

A change is billable when it adds or alters:

- scope or deliverables;
- integrations or environments;
- users, volume, languages, or jurisdictions;
- security, compliance, audit, or availability requirements;
- deadlines or priority;
- IP, source-code, exclusivity, or resale rights;
- support, maintenance, training, or documentation;
- customer dependencies or assumptions.

No verbal request changes the agreed scope. Record it, price it, and obtain
approval.

## 8. Discount and exception approval

The following require an explicit written decision by Benjamin Gerrit Hoff:

- a discount above 15%;
- a free pilot or bespoke commercial deliverable;
- work below the minimum engagement;
- a public-institution humanitarian waiver;
- source-code transfer, buyout, exclusivity, or white-label rights;
- pure-equity or pure-revenue-share delivery without current funding;
- a Common Good exception where eligibility is uncertain;
- any arrangement that may affect HUB_Optimus ownership, control, or reputation.

The decision must identify the reason, scope, duration, consideration, risks, and
whether it creates precedent. The default is that it creates no precedent.

## 9. Common Good intake

Before approving pro bono work, record:

- verified mission and legal status;
- direct human or animal benefit;
- hidden-beneficiary and commercial-benefit check;
- data and safeguarding assessment;
- scope and maximum capacity;
- direct costs and sponsor, if any;
- noncommercial license;
- responsible contact;
- misuse and termination boundary;
- owner approval.

## 10. Handoff rule for AI and operators

An AI system or operator must not:

- promise free work to a company or public body;
- invent a discount or price exception;
- imply that public visibility makes HUB_Optimus free for commercial use;
- promise source-code ownership, exclusivity, joint-venture rights, or unlimited
  support;
- classify an organization as Common Good solely because it calls itself an NGO;
- use beneficiary suffering as a sales story;
- continue bespoke work after the free qualification boundary without a paid or
  approved pro bono engagement.

When classification is unclear, stop commercial design work and obtain an owner
or contracting decision.
