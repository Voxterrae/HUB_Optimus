# HUB_Optimus — First Paid Customer Launch Plan v0.1

**Marketplace dependency:** issue `#1865`

## 1. Objective

Secure the first external paid institutional relationship while continuing to
complete, test, and productize HUB_Optimus and LCDH-OS.

The launch does not wait for every future module to be complete. It does require
that every capability offered to a customer is factually classified, scoped,
secured, priced, and supported.

## 2. Two synchronized lanes

### Product lane

- complete the LCDH-OS client-zero implementation and evidence;
- inventory every application, connector, API, iframe/embedded surface, control
  plane, and operational dependency;
- classify each item as concept, prototype, tested, pilot-ready, or production-
  ready;
- harden identity, audit, rollback, telemetry, metering, tenant isolation, and
  support boundaries;
- remove demo-only behavior and undocumented manual dependencies;
- package reusable platform capability separately from customer-specific data
  and configuration.

### Commercial lane

- publish a controlled `Request Institutional Access` path;
- qualify authority, budget, beneficiary, procurement, data, and requested
  rights before bespoke work;
- prepare the evidence and trust pack;
- prepare a paid Institutional Diagnostic and a paid Governed Proof of Value;
- prepare proposal, contracting, IP, DPA, SLA, procurement, and clean-payment
  materials for professional review;
- prepare Microsoft Marketplace/private-offer readiness while retaining direct
  B2B, AWS, and Merchant-of-Record alternatives;
- build a maximum-ten strategic opportunity map without mass cold prospecting.

Neither lane may represent an untested capability as ready.

## 3. Initial institutional wedge

The first offer is:

> **HUB_Optimus Governed Microsoft 365 & Enterprise Automation Program**

The strongest initial buyer profile uses Microsoft 365, Azure, Entra,
SharePoint, Outlook, Power Platform, Dataverse, or Microsoft Graph and has a
material problem involving email, documents, approvals, CRM, evidence,
automation, auditability, or governance.

Preferred initial verticals:

1. real-estate groups, property developers, property managers, and adjacent
   professional-services firms where LCDH-OS creates relevant evidence;
2. Microsoft partners and integrators that need a governed product layer rather
   than another low-margin subcontractor;
3. evidence-heavy or regulated organizations with an existing Microsoft
   procurement path and a funded operational problem.

## 4. Entry offer

### Institutional Diagnostic

- commercial range: EUR 60,000–120,000 excluding tax;
- begins only after signed order, complete procurement pack, and cleared payment;
- output: current-state map, evidence/risk model, target architecture, security
  and governance boundaries, implementation plan, product/SKU map, acceptance
  criteria, and commercial conversion proposal;
- excludes production deployment, unrestricted source code, ownership transfer,
  white-label, OEM, resale, and exclusivity.

### Governed Proof of Value

- commercial range: EUR 250,000–500,000 excluding tax;
- covers one high-value workflow or operating domain;
- uses controlled data and measurable acceptance criteria;
- includes audit, rollback, evidence, productization review, and conversion plan;
- is never a free bespoke proof of concept.

### Conversion

```text
Institutional Diagnostic       EUR 60,000–120,000
        ↓
Governed Proof of Value        EUR 250,000–500,000
        ↓
Implementation                 EUR 750,000–1,500,000 initial target
        ↓
Recurring platform value       EUR 300,000–750,000 ARR
```

Indicative first-year value of one successful anchor relationship:

> **EUR 1.36 million to EUR 2.87 million**, excluding exceptional sovereign,
> white-label, OEM, resale, or high-usage rights.

## 5. Institutional access gate

A request must not enter custom design until the deterministic qualification
process has classified it.

Paid qualification requires, at minimum:

- identifiable legal organization;
- actual operational problem and beneficiary;
- decision authority or a funded route to it;
- budget at or above the applicable minimum;
- acceptance that diagnosis and Proof of Value are paid;
- procurement channel or credible readiness path;
- no demand for free bespoke work;
- no implied ownership, control, source-code transfer, white-label, resale, or
  exclusivity.

Common Good requests follow a separate nonprofit and anti-exploitation review.

## 6. Evidence and trust pack

The first pack must contain only supported claims:

- one-page institutional overview;
- founder, ownership, and IP boundary;
- product and architecture map;
- LCDH-OS client-zero case study, redacted and permission-safe;
- capability/readiness inventory;
- identity, tenant, security, audit, rollback, and data-boundary overview;
- applications, connectors, APIs, embedded, support, and commercial rights map;
- repository evidence and check summary;
- Common Good doctrine;
- financial-sector trust-roadmap summary;
- explicit limitations and items still under construction.

## 7. Opportunity map

Maintain no more than ten active institutions. Each entry needs:

- institution and sector;
- real operational problem;
- likely budget and first-year contract value;
- decision-maker or trusted introduction;
- technology and procurement alignment;
- unique HUB_Optimus advantage;
- product-readiness fit;
- free-work or rights-transfer risks;
- next owner-approved action.

The opportunity map is a strategic access map, not a mass cold-sales list.

## 8. Operating rule for AI-assisted work

AI-assisted execution occurs only in active sessions and must leave visible,
reviewable artifacts in GitHub, SharePoint, or another approved system. There is
no invisible asynchronous work.

Each active session should:

1. inspect the current issues and evidence;
2. complete the highest-value unblocked task;
3. validate it;
4. record what changed and what remains;
5. identify any owner, legal, accounting, tax, security, or customer action that
   cannot be automated.

## 9. Launch readiness gates

The first paid proposal may be issued when:

- one institutional offer is fully scoped and priced;
- the offered capability is at least pilot-ready;
- the evidence pack contains no unsupported claims;
- commercial, IP, data, support, procurement, and payment boundaries are clear;
- delivery capacity and owner participation are available;
- the contracting entity, bank, accountant/tax adviser, and legal review path are
  selected;
- no free bespoke work or rights transfer is implied.

Production deployment requires a separate readiness decision.
